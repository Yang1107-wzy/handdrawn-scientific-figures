# Four composition templates

Use one template as a starting point; adapt spatial allocation to the content. Append the fixed visual language from [the master prompt](master-prompt.md).

## 1. Research scene

Draw [environment] with [actual entities]. Make [main interaction] the focal point. Show [observations or geometric references] with concise annotations. Use a cutaway only when it explains otherwise hidden structure. Leave white space at [relevant positions] for exact labels. Label the scene conceptual unless it is an actual source photograph. Observation rays must not become unsupported causal arrows.

## 2. Method framework

Represent [verified inputs], [transformations] and [outputs] with subject-specific illustrations and independently typeset labels. Make [central module] dominant. Show the exact directed relations [edge list]. Route [feedback] around the composition to [specific stage]. Do not add a neural network, loss function, verification step or deployment outcome unless the method supplies one.

## 3. Mechanism and technical pathway

Visualize [mechanism] at [appropriate physical or conceptual scale]. Show [relevant states or geometric entities] and the relation [source-supported relation]. Use zoomed detail, cutaway, spatial map or time sequence according to the mechanism. Preserve coordinate references and temporal order. Put equations and exact variables in later vector layers.

## 4. Illustration plus data

Use a small explanatory scene for [phenomenon] and a quantitative panel for [question]. Generate only the conceptual illustration. Plot [actual CSV or identified aggregate values] with a regular numerical font and exact axes. State [units, denominator, protocol]. Keep the art separate from measured data. Do not invent sample points, intervals, trajectories or distributions. If the example is synthetic teaching data, declare that in the caption and source record.

## Evidence boundary

Pictures explain mechanisms; data show results. A screenshot is a supplied source asset, not something to recreate as evidence. A generated surface, point cloud or room is conceptual unless measured coordinates were actually used to draw it. Do not convert an attractive example into evidence.
